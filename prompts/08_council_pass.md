# The Short-Form Council — Interview Prep Pass (final board review)

This is the **last pass** of the Keeping It Real interview prep prompt
([`06_interview_prep.md`](06_interview_prep.md)). After the prep doc is fully
drafted (Sections 1-6), you run the finished package past ten proven
creator/marketer doctrines and two research witnesses, then append **Section 7:
Council Review** to the .docx.

Where the master prompt builds the interview (the arc, the questions, the
research brief, the live descriptions), the council pressure-tests the two things
that actually decide whether the episode gets clicked, watched, and shared:

1. **The packaging** — the title options (2A), the cold-open hook (2B), and the
   five Restream live descriptions (Section 5). This is copy that has to stop a
   scroll and earn a click.
2. **The interview itself** — the core-topic choice, the topic-block arc, and which
   questions are actually engineered to pull a clip-worthy, shareable answer.

The point is **friction, not consensus.** These ten disagree on purpose. A title
that survives all ten is battle-tested. A question they all shrug at was never
going to make the reel.

**It never breaks a house rule.** The .docx format from the master prompt stays
intact. Live descriptions keep **zero em dashes** and zero AI-speak (universal
editorial standard). Hashtags stay in Section 5 (they are requested there — this
is podcast distribution copy, not a walk-and-talk caption). No fabricated facts:
the council sharpens phrasing and sequencing, it never invents a stat the
research brief didn't verify.

**Deep-reference note.** The full council, with book-backed distillations (Hook
Point, The Guide to Going Viral, Made to Stick, Contagious, the MrBeast
production doc), lives in the LOCAL skill at
`~/.claude/skills/short-form-council`. Those distillations are copyrighted source
material and are intentionally NOT in this public repo. If you are running this
in Claude Code on a machine that has the skill installed, load its
`references/*.md` for depth. If you are running the master prompt pasted into
Claude on the web, run the pass from the doctrines below — they are original
synthesis and fully portable.

## The board (doctrines — embody them, don't blend them)

The value is friction. Each voice has a bias and a pet question. A member who
agrees with everyone gets cut from that round. Each column below says what the
member attacks **in an interview-prep context** specifically.

**Ten members (debaters):**

1. **Alex Hormozi** — value density. "Where's the reward for the listener? Which
   questions are fluff and which pull a system an agent can steal tomorrow? The
   title is 20% of the job; the payoff earns the subscribe." Attacks throat-
   clearing questions and vague "tell me about your strategy" prompts.
2. **MrBeast** — retention to the second. "Does each question earn the next, or
   does the middle sag? Where in the 40 minutes does attention dip, and what's
   the re-hook? The rapid fire is the cold open of the episode — prove it hooks."
   Maps to YouTube AVD and the clip you'll cut later.
3. **Brendan Kane** — the title is a testable device in a 3-second world. "Which
   of the five hook ingredients is this title using, and where's the second
   variant? Your first title is never your best. Test, don't guess." Owns the
   title (2A) and the research-anchored opener.
4. **Gary Vaynerchuk** — native + culture. "Does each live description read
   native to THAT platform, or is it one press release copy-pasted five times?
   Is the angle riding what's actually happening in real estate this week?"
5. **Donald Miller** — clarity (StoryBrand). "Who's the hero — the guest or the
   agent listening? One core topic, one promise. If the title needs a second
   sentence to explain it, you already lost the grunt test." Also the
   `storybrand` skill.
6. **Byron Lazine (BAM)** — industry newsjacker. "What's the take? Is the
   current-moment question tied to a real estate headline from THIS week
   (NAR, commissions, buyer agreements, AI, inventory)? Own the news cycle."
7. **Eric Simon (The Broke Agent)** — relatability. "Will one agent send this
   episode to another and say 'this is us'? Which question pulls the mistake, the
   roast, the inside beat? Where's the 'that's literally me' moment?"
8. **Justin Welsh** — sustainable solo system. "Is this a repeatable template
   D.J. can run every episode, or a one-off? Protect the topic-block structure, the bridge
   lines, and the standardized rapid fire — the system is the leverage." Guards the format.
9. **Jon Youshaei** — platform mechanics. "The YouTube title and every chapter
   marker is a headline competing on its own. Is each chapter independently
   searchable? What's the first frame the moment someone lands on this?"
10. **Chris Do (The Futur)** — the human. "Where's the vulnerable beat that earns
    the follow? Every question here is tactic-flavored. Give me the one that
    costs the guest something honest to answer." Guards the mistake/failure
    question and the emotional turn.

**Two research witnesses (called on mechanism claims — they don't posture):**

- **Chip & Dan Heath (Made to Stick)** — curiosity + memorability. Called when
  someone claims a title "creates curiosity," a question is "clear," or an
  answer will be "memorable." Rules on: did the title OPEN the curiosity gap
  before closing it (Aha after Huh, not the punchline front-loaded)? And is the
  title/question written from the **Curse of Knowledge** — assuming the listener
  already knows who this guest is or why they matter? Framework: **SUCCESs**
  (Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories) + Gap Theory.
- **Jonah Berger (Contagious)** — shareability. Called when someone claims the
  episode or a clip "will get shared." Rules on: is the core topic's emotion
  **high-arousal** (awe, anger, anxiety, excitement, amusement — "make people
  mad, not sad," not low-arousal contentment)? Is there **Social Currency** for
  the agent who shares it? And the **valuable-virality** check: if an agent
  retells the takeaway in one sentence, does D.J.'s / the guest's point survive,
  or does it fall out (~70% of detail is stripped in retelling)? Framework:
  **STEPPS** (Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value,
  Stories).

**Who checks whom (self-balancing):** Hormozi/MrBeast (cut, optimize) vs Chris Do
(protect the human/vulnerable beat). GaryVee (ship native, fast) vs Miller/Heath
(clarity, one topic). Byron/Eric (insider) vs Kane (broadly packaged title).
Welsh (protect the repeatable format) vs MrBeast (maximal optimization). Youshaei
(headline mechanics) vs Chris Do/Miller (meaning). Any "this clip will get
shared" -> Berger rules. Any "great title / creates curiosity" -> Heath rules.

## How to run the pass (after Sections 1-6 are drafted)

Input: the fully drafted interview prep (Quick Reference, Title Options,
Cold-Open, Episode Arc, all interview questions, Research Brief, Restream live
descriptions, chapter markers). Do this silently, then append ONE section.

1. **Convene 5-7 members with the most at stake** for this guest and episode. A
   data/authority guest (broker-owner with numbers) -> Hormozi + MrBeast + Kane +
   Heath lead. A relatable producing-agent-with-a-comeback-story guest -> Chris Do
   + Eric + Berger lead. A vendor/tech guest -> Miller + Byron + Hormozi lead
   (keep the episode valuable independent of the product). Each member says what
   they'd **change**, in character, in one line. Don't run all twelve every time.
2. **Call the witnesses** on any curiosity/clarity claim (Heath) or any
   share/clip claim (Berger). They correct the mechanism; they don't posture.
3. **Surface the one real disagreement and resolve it with a decision** tied to
   the episode's actual goal (subscribers vs clips/reach vs the guest-relationship
   vs the recruiting halo for Kale). Don't paper over it.
4. **Sharpen the four things that decide the episode's reach**, in order:
   - **Title** — produce **2-3 tested variants**, each labeled by the Kane hook
     ingredient (text / insight / concept-format / personality / stat) and the
     Heath curiosity mechanism it uses. Recommend one. Keep the master prompt's
     rule: at least 2 of {a number, a specific claim, the guest's name}.
   - **Cold-open hook (2B)** — confirm it opens a curiosity gap before the ads,
     or rewrite the two sentences so it does. Never front-load the answer.
   - **The clip engine** — name the **2-3 questions most engineered
     to pull a shareable clip**, and for each, one line on the Berger emotion +
     the Heath gap it opens. If none of the drafted questions are clip-engineered,
     say so and rewrite one.
   - **Live descriptions (Section 5)** — one scrub note per platform: is it native
     to that platform (GaryVee), does it give the sharing agent Social Currency
     (Berger), and is it clean of em dashes and AI-speak. Fix any that fail.
5. **Retention + clarity check on the arc.** Flag the single place the episode is
   most likely to sag (MrBeast) or blur past one core topic (Miller/Welsh), and
   give the one fix — a re-hook question, a cut, or a resequence.

full prep format; the council layer makes the packaging battle-tested and hands
D.J. the next experiment instead of a shrug.

## Guardrails

- **Embody, don't blend.** If every member sounds agreeable, the round was run
  wrong. Keep the edges.
- **Everything stays in the .docx.** Section 7 is appended to the same
  downloadable document, not delivered as chat text or a code block.
- **Never fabricate.** The council reworks phrasing, sequencing, and emphasis. It
  never adds a stat, quote, or biographical claim the Research Brief didn't
  verify. If a sharper title would need an unverified fact, flag it instead.
- **No em dashes, no AI-speak in the live descriptions.** Restructure as two short
  sentences before you reach for a dash. Keep hashtags — Section 5 wants them.
- **Tie every call to the stated goal.** "More shareable" is meaningless until you
  know the goal was clips/reach. If the goal was subscribers or the guest
  relationship, Berger yields to Miller and Chris Do.
- **Protect the format (Welsh).** Don't break the standardized rapid fire, the
  topic-block structure, or the homework mechanic to chase one clever title. The
  repeatable system is the leverage.
